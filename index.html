Terpene builder.

About 30,000 terpenoids are known, and every one is assembled from the same two C5 units, IPP and DMAPP. Join them head to tail, follow a carbocation through a terpene synthase, then find the C5 units hidden in finished molecules.

The prenyltransferase reaction

Every chain starts with DMAPP. Its head is C1, the carbon carrying the diphosphate (OPP); its tail is the branched end. Step through one round of chain elongation. Carbons are coloured by the C5 unit they came from and numbered as in their own unit.

How does the enzyme know when to stop?

The chain grows at the diphosphate end, next to the Mg2+ ions. Each round pushes the older units one step deeper into a hydrophobic tunnel. When the tail reaches the floor of the tunnel there is no room for another round, and the product is released. Pick an enzyme and add IPP.

Head to head

The two largest classes are made by joining two whole chains at their heads (a 1′-1 joint) instead of adding IPP.

Cis or trans: rubber and gutta-percha

GPP, FPP and GGPP synthases make trans (E) double bonds. The rubber transferase on rubber particles in Hevea latex makes cis (Z) ones, starting from FPP. Try it with the rubber transferase above.

The geometry decides the material. Cis kinks stop the chains packing, so natural rubber stays elastic. Gutta-percha, the latex of Palaquium gutta from this region, is the same polyisoprene with trans double bonds: its chains pack into crystals, so it is hard. It insulated the first undersea telegraph cables in the 1850s.

Two ways to make the first cation

Class I enzymes, including the prenyltransferases and every synthase on this page, start by pulling off the diphosphate. Three Mg2+ ions bound to an aspartate-rich DDxxD motif do the pulling.

Class II enzymes start by protonating a double bond (or an epoxide) with a catalytic aspartate. Copalyl diphosphate synthases use this to begin gibberellins and pine resin acids such as pimaric acid. Oxidosqualene cyclases use it to fold the C30 chain into sterols.

Why are there so many terpenes?

Plant genomes carry dozens of terpene synthase genes, roughly 40 to 150. Each active site is a mould that holds the substrate in one shape, shields the cation from water and places a base where one particular proton should leave.

The moulds are not perfect. Most monoterpene synthases make one main product plus a few side products, and some sesquiterpene synthases barely choose at all: γ-humulene synthase from grand fir makes 52 different products from FPP.

After the synthase: same limonene, two mints

Peppermint and spearmint make the same (−)-limonene. Their cytochrome P450 limonene hydroxylases then add oxygen to different carbons, and the pathways part. A single substitution, Phe363 to Ile, converts the spearmint enzyme into one that hydroxylates the peppermint position. The literature numbers these positions C3 and C6 (p-menthane numbering); here they keep their GPP numbers, C1 and C4.

Find the C5 units

Cut every bond that joins one C5 unit to another, as the wavy lines do in the textbook figure. Tap a C–C bond to cut it, and tap again to rejoin. Every piece must be one isoprene unit: five carbons, branched like isopentane. Oxygen and nitrogen atoms do not count. Pieces with five carbons are coloured as you go.
